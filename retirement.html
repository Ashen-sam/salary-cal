Illustrative estimate only. The Public Servants' Pension Scheme (PSPS) is a non-contributory, defined-benefit scheme where the monthly pension scales with years of service, reaching roughly 85–90% of last drawn salary after 30 years — this tool applies that published range on a straight-line basis for years below 30. It does not replicate the exact statutory formula, which is set out in Pension Minutes and circulars issued by the Department of Pensions and depends on your specific service category (civil, teaching, police, armed forces, etc). Confirm your exact figure with your HR/establishments division or the Department of Pensions.
Also Applicable to You
  • Widows'/Widowers' & Orphans' Pension (W&OP) — a separate contributory deduction from your salary throughout service, securing a survivor pension for your dependents. The rate depends on your salary grade/circular, so it isn't included in the figure above.
  • Retiring gratuity — public officers typically receive a lump sum at retirement calculated under pension commutation rules (part of your pension exchanged for cash), a different mechanism from the private-sector Gratuity Act formula, so no figure is estimated here.
  • EPF/ETF do not apply to permanent pensionable public officers.
Sources: EPF Act No. 15 of 1958 & ETF Act No. 46 of 1980 (contribution rates); Central Bank of Sri Lanka / EPF Fund Management report (10.75% rate declared on balances at end of 2025); Payment of Gratuity Act No. 12 of 1983; Department of Pensions Sri Lanka & published research on the Public Servants' Pension Scheme (85–90% of last salary after 30 years). This tool gives planning estimates, not a certified benefits statement — always confirm exact figures with the CBSL EPF Department, your employer, or the Department of Pensions before making financial decisions.
