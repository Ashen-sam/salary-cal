EPF & ETF — Private Sector Only

Statutory retirement contributions. Calculated on total monthly earnings (basic + allowances that are part of regular pay, per employer's contract terms).

EPF — Employee contribution 8%
EPF — Employer contribution 12%
EPF — Total contribution 20%
ETF — Employer contribution 3%
ETF — Employee contribution None (0%)
EPF is mandatory from day one of private-sector employment (Employees' Provident Fund Act No. 15 of 1958). ETF is an employer-only contribution (Employees' Trust Fund Act No. 46 of 1980) — nothing is deducted from the employee's pay for it.
Government Sector — Pension Instead of EPF/ETF

Permanent, pensionable public officers are not covered by the EPF/ETF Acts. Their retirement security comes from a separate government pension framework instead.

Public Servants' Pension Scheme (PSPS)
  • Non-contributory defined-benefit pension, funded entirely from the government budget (no monthly deduction from salary for this part).
  • Eligible after 10 years of service in a permanent, pensionable post.
  • Payable from age 55 (men) / 50 (women), or at the retirement age set for the service.
  • Monthly pension is a proportion of last drawn salary based on years served — reaching roughly 85–90% of final salary after 30 years of service, under the applicable pension formula.
Widows'/Widowers' & Orphans' Pension (W&OP)
  • Contributory survivor-benefit scheme — a portion is deducted from the officer's monthly salary throughout service, under the Widows' & Orphans' Pension Fund Ordinance (and the Widowers' & Orphans' Pension Act for female officers).
  • Provides a monthly pension to the surviving spouse and eligible children (under 26, unemployed, unmarried) after the officer's death.
  • The exact deduction rate depends on salary scale/grade and is set out in the officer's pension circular — it is administered separately from PAYE/APIT and EPF/ETF, so no single flat percentage applies to every grade.
Gratuity
  • A lump-sum payment made at retirement, calculated against years of service and final salary rules — separate from the monthly pension.
APIT income tax (above) still applies to government salaries exactly as it does to private sector salaries — only EPF/ETF are private-sector-specific.
